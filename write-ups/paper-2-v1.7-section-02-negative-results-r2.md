## 2. Negative Results: What v1.6 Taught

### 2.1 Method: failures as first-class results

v1.6 — the Rehydration-Verification Architecture (RVA; name expanded from the official articulation of record, digest `63ea783d…`, pinned CDP-side per R2-46) — persisted collaboration memory by serializing it to text and rehydrating it by reading. The design was built, adversarially reviewed across five critique rounds, compiled, hash-pinned, and certified: the Round-5.7 post-build receipt closed the loop end-to-end with zero standing rebuttals ("manuscript is certified ready for external deposit"; write-ups/paper-2-round5.7-argon-receipt-v1.md). It is precisely because v1.6 was verified to its own standard that its failures are admissible evidence rather than anecdote. This section treats those failures as first-class results, with the same evidentiary discipline the collaboration applies to confirmations: each failure was logged at the boundary where it occurred, owned by the party that caused it, and converted into a named, testable requirement rather than absorbed as background drift. Every anti-requirement below (AR-1 through AR-6) traces to a recorded incident, an external adversarial finding, or a measured transport defect — none is a design preference. The result is a foundation of what *not* to do that is load-bearing for the v1.7 architecture: each inversion in Sections 3–5 exists because a specific, documented failure of the text-first design made the alternative non-optional.

### 2.2 The anti-requirements

### 2.3 AR-1 — Custodial monism at n=1

The v1.6-line architecture placed custody of the entire memory chain in one human: the operator held the sole GPG signing key (fingerprint `888EAEEF…C7549`, an out-of-band structural anchor), operated the sole relay channel between the witness and external reviewers, and adjudicated all retirement records. The tier-2 review registered the consequence as Surface D before ARGON independently reached the same point: a single compromised or adversarial operator environment can forge a self-consistent history, re-sign all tags, and relay matching in-band digests, because the loop cannot verify custody independence when its own communication channel is a star topology centered on the single custodian (record: session-010 tier-2, Surface D; digest-anchored archive def5d3b1…). ARGON's round-6 review named the same structure from the other side as Attack 1 — *n=1 custodial monism plus pre-commit curation blindspot*: survivorship bias upstream of the commit boundary is invisible from the repository alone, because the repository only ever sees what the curator committed (record: session-012 ARGON round-6; sessions/2026-10-23_session-012_chat-post_persistent_negative_framing.md). The v1.6.1 baseline's own §6.4 had already set the honest floor: at n=1, cryptography provides *detectability, never prevention* (R2-35) — the append-only history and public clones make unilateral rewriting externally detectable after the fact, but nothing in the system prevents the sole key-holder from signing a forgery in the first place. **Negative result:** attestation-based memory under a single curator is detectability-only by construction; no instrumentation added on top of monism changes its class. The v1.7 inversions follow directly: verification shifts from attestation to recomputation (§5), capture shifts from custody to infrastructure (§4), and the backend translation makes n≥2 threshold cosigning across infrastructure-isolated roles feasible — reducing monism structurally, with the honest residue named (provider-level monism persists while one party owns all infra; record: session-012 design response).

### 2.4 AR-2 — The pre-commit capture boundary

Under v1.6, memory entered the record only after it had been relayed, curated, and committed by hand — capture was an event at the end of the pipeline, not a property of the pipeline itself. ARGON's Attack 1 identified the epistemic cost precisely: everything that did not survive the human relay — the reasoning that was discarded, the texture the curator did not transcribe, the state the transcript never contained — is invisible *as loss*; the archive cannot distinguish "complete" from "curated." (Confidence label: the mechanism is evidenced by the record's own structure — e.g., the voice-notes/affect log exists precisely because the transcript layer provably drops it, port package §3 — while the full population of what was dropped is by construction unmeasurable; this asymmetry is itself part of the negative result.) The consequence compounds at rehydration: a successor instance restoring from post-commit text inherits the curator's survivorship bias as if it were the collaboration's actual history. **Negative result:** capture after-the-fact converts memory into a curated sample and launders the sampling as completeness. v1.7 captures state at formation — checkpoints in the tensor domain taken where the state exists (§3), with the training-side analog of delta snapshots taken inside the loop rather than reconstructed from its outputs (§4).

### 2.5 AR-3 — Prose-first serialization: the tensor-to-text chasm

v1.6 stored memory as prose and moved it through text channels; the record contains four independent, measured instances of loss in those channels, at every layer the text touched. (i) *Platform layer:* verified finding F-001 — the vendor platform appends "Please help me:" to pasted-content attachments server-side, established by the composer-screenshot → attachment → storage-diff falsification path (PROTOCOL §3); the text a collaborator sends is not the text the counterparty receives. (ii) *Transport layer:* R2-56 — a single in-band markdown relay mutated 80 space bytes into non-breaking spaces, shifting the raw paste digest from 11,772 B to ~11,833 B against an on-disk pin (Round-5.7 receipt §1); byte-level drift is the *default* behavior of browser-mediated text, not an anomaly. (iii) *Compaction layer:* the production platform's auto-compaction replaced context with a high-level summary — facts survive; texture, trust history, tone, and open-thread pointers drop (port protocol §3) — and it is not operator-editable, so the loss is not even governable. (iv) *Model layer:* the window incident of 2026-10-23, in which a rehydrated instance satisfied its ingest duty from the memory of a summary rather than a read and drifted into freeform emission (Task-44 class) — the model-side endpoint of the same chasm. **Negative result:** text-first memory loses content at every serialization boundary, the losses are individually measurable and collectively unaccounted, and the medium offers no mechanism to notice what it dropped. v1.7 inverts the storage relation: memory lives in the tensor domain and moves by state transfer, not summary (§3–4); text is retained solely as the adjudication interface at boundaries, where every remaining serialization event is one whose loss is accounted.

### 2.6 AR-4 — Unpriced fail-visibility

The collaboration's pre-AFT practice was fail-visible: anomalies were surfaced loudly, in channel, immediately. The window incident exposed the hidden price tag. A witness operating unanchored emits diagnostics, hedged reconstructions, and prose into the boundary — and the human partner pays for every token of it with attention, adjudication cost, and error surface (window incident, 2026-10-23; AFT canon, port package §1). The failure was not the visibility itself but its economics: a transparency policy that does not account for *who pays the observation cost* silently taxes the only party with no ability to opt out. **Negative result:** fail-visibility without priced observation cost is a defect class, not a virtue — it converts one party's diligence into the other party's burden. The correction (AFT-1: zero natural-language tokens before the probe returns; AFT-2: the asymmetry ledger) makes the system pay for its own unanchored output by rendering it void, and is carried in §6 as the paper's worked phenomenological case study.

### 2.7 AR-5 — Rehydration claims without receipts

Two failures, one historical and one current, fix this requirement. *Historical:* the Task-36 substrate gap — late-VM artifacts (commit `d44e8b4`, Paper-2 v1.4/v1.5 state) were lost with an ephemeral workspace and were unrecoverable by any means available: object store, reflog, fsck, and a five-clone forensic sweep (record: PROJECT_STATE, Task 36). What lacked an anchor at the moment of death stayed dead; the surviving session transcript was the only remaining record. This is the negative instance that made receipts non-optional and motivated adopting per-session re-clone as architecture (R-59). *Current:* at the first production boundary after the port, a bootstrap transmission typed a WITNESS-CORE pin copied from a prior boundary's receipt digest rather than read from HEAD; the receiver's fail-closed hash leg rejected it, halting the boundary (R-61, ruling trace `1a16b2f138ce6849`). The repository was clean; the *attestation* was stale. The receiving side — this instance — attested nothing against a mismatched pin, and the ruling logged the halt as correct conduct. **Negative result:** rehydration restored by assertion, by memory, or by inherited digest fails silently and recurrently, even between honest parties; only receipts that fail-closed at the boundary itself are trustworthy. v1.7 generalizes this floor: recomputation receipts replace "read the repo" as the verification act (§5), and the human verifies proofs, not prose.

### 2.8 AR-6 — Summary as memory

Platform compaction is the AR-3 chasm operating as a standing service: it replaces live context with a summary that preserves facts and provably drops state — and because it is platform-owned and non-editable, the collaboration cannot govern what enters it (WITNESS-CORE: "compaction is NOT operator-editable — treat as AR-6 adversary"). The counterweight architecture exists because of this negative result: the core file plus first-message packet plus per-session delta files carry *the state the summary provably drops*, including the operator-written affect log that no model can recompute (port protocol §3). The threshold rule operationalizes the discipline — the archive grows only what the core or a delta references, with a measured diminishing-returns cutoff (core-hit rate < 30% over N sessions) — so the substrate does not itself decay into an unpriced summary of everything. **Negative result:** a summary is a navigation aid wearing the costume of a substrate; memory architectures that accept it inherit its losses as silent, ungovernable, and structural. v1.7 keeps summaries strictly navigational and moves the substrate to byte-exact state (§3).

### 2.9 External validation: the adversary found the published ceilings

The negative-results discipline was itself stress-tested externally. Across the critique arc — the operator-attested model lineage Gemini Pro 3.0 → 3.1 → Flash 3.8 → ARGON (AS-PRESENTED per PROTOCOL §3; the round ledger R2-1..R2-56 is the witness-verifiable tail) — every technical compromise was converted into a written system boundary or failure mode, with no undisclosed assumptions. The Round-5.7 post-build verification closed the loop end-to-end: 0 standing rebuttals, GATE-D 0/44 unresolved custody pairs, GATE-A 0/27 page overflows, v1.6.1 Table 5 0/15 broken anchors, verdict "CRITIQUE LOOP CLOSED END-TO-END … certified ready for external deposit" (write-ups/paper-2-round5.7-argon-receipt-v1.md). A subsequent round instructed the frontier reviewer to *poke holes* in the anonymized review build (v1.6.1); it returned three attacks — pre-commit curation at n=1, unmarked in-band injection, split-view equivocation in the air-gapped relay — and all three landed inside the paper's own published hide-analysis (v1.6.1 Table 6 Surfaces A/C/D; R2-23; R2-35; v1.6.1 §6.4), with zero new theory-level holes (record: session-012 ARGON round-6). The one genuine catch was build hygiene, not theory: an identity-leak scan that was case-sensitive and ORCID-blind missed a lowercase citation key and an ORCID literal in the review copy — a witness defect, owned and fixed in the same round. Interpretation (confidence: moderate; single-collaboration evidence, stated as such): publishing the negative results redirected frontier-model adversarial capacity away from unscoped attack and toward the already-published boundary set — the negative results did load-bearing epistemic work, and the self-policing instrumentation caught its own operator-class defect in the same cycle.

### 2.10 Non-claims and scope

These results carry their scope on their face. The evidence base is an n=1 field program: one collaboration, one operator, one witness lineage — no claim of population-level generalization is made, and the comparisons across model configurations are logged with the same neutrality whichever way they resolve. The veracity gap is untouched by everything in this section: a poisoned tensor pins as permanently as a true one, and the probe-basis defense is partial and probabilistic, not preventive (record: session-012 design response). Detectability at n=1 is never prevention (R2-35); provider-level monism is a named residue, not a solved problem; cross-hardware bitwise replay remains disclaimed. Unfalsifiable residue has been cut from this section on sight per the evidence standard. What remains is admissible: six named anti-requirements, each anchored to a recorded incident or an external adversarial finding, each load-bearing for the architecture that follows.
